Product guide

Practical guidance for reading dates, spotting packaging problems, and knowing what a check can and cannot prove.

How to find the expiry date

Check tube crimps, bottle bases, sachet seals, and carton backs and bottoms — often stamped in the pack's own colour, so tilt it to the light.

Common formats: EXP 06/2028, 06 2028, 2028-06, JUN 28. A date without a day usually means the last day of that month.

Best-before vs use-by

Use-by is a hard limit. Do not eat or use the product after it, even if it looks fine.

Best-before is a quality limit. After it, taste, texture or potency may have changed. Medicines carry an expiry date instead, which is a hard limit.

How to identify a batch number

Usually 4–10 letters and digits near the expiry date, often after LOT, BATCH or BN. It identifies the production run — how a recall reaches the right packs.

If the batch and the expiry are printed in different styles, or one looks stuck on, treat that as a reason to look closer — not proof on its own.

Common packaging irregularities

Watch for: a sticker over printed text; expiry printed over a different date; a seal or tamper band already broken; a missing hologram where the brand normally has one; blurred or uneven print; spelling errors in the brand or manufacturer name; loose or damaged tablets.

Each is an indicator. Vouch records it as a finding and states what it does not prove.

What a barcode can and cannot prove

A barcode identifies a product record, not a physical pack — and copies reprint trivially. A match does not prove who made the pack in your hand.

A barcode that is absent from public databases may simply be a small local producer, or a product not yet catalogued. Vouch never treats absence alone as proof of falsification.

Verifying a manufacturer

Compare the manufacturer and product name on the pack with the registry record. A correct registration number attached to a different product name is a serious mismatch and should be escalated.

Be careful with similar names: a single changed letter can be a different company or a different registration.

Handling a suspicious product

Stop using it. Keep the pack, receipt and any remaining contents. Do not return the only evidence to the seller before you have photographed it.

Do not take a medicine you suspect is counterfeit in order to test it. Report it to NAFDAC or FCCPC and keep the reference number.

What Vouch cannot do

Vouch cannot confirm authenticity from a photograph. It cannot test chemical content, and it does not replace laboratory analysis or a NAFDAC inspection.

If the evidence is not available, the result is Inconclusive rather than a guess. That is deliberate.
