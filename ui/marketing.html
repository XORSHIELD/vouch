The gap we work in

The rules exist. The last metre is missing.

A registration number on a pack is not a check. An expiry date printed in a different ink is not a check. Most people have no practical way to look anything up while standing in a shop — and no clear path if the answer is wrong.

Vouch closes that gap with two honest rails and one follow-through.

Two rails

Every product on the shelf is not the same problem

Medicines are regulated through a public register. Food and cosmetics are regulated too, but their registrations are not published the same way. Vouch says which rail it is on.

Rail one · NAFDAC register

Medicines, devices, vaccines, herbals, veterinary

The printed registration number is resolved against the live NAFDAC Greenbook, including the numbering variants and OCR confusions that make genuine packs look unregistered.

  • Registration status and expiry
  • Product name and manufacturer comparison
  • Inactive registrations kept distinct from falsified packs
Rail two · Product databases

Food, drink, water, cosmetics, household

The barcode is checked against open product databases, with check-digit validation before any lookup. The record tells you what the product claims to be — not who made the pack in your hand.

  • EAN-8, UPC, EAN-13 and GTIN-14 validation
  • Open Food Facts, Open Beauty Facts, Open Products Facts
  • Clear language when a code is not found

Questions

Straight answers

Does a match prove the product is genuine?
No. A registration record or barcode match identifies a product or licence; it does not prove that a specific pack was made by that manufacturer. Vouch separates “the record exists and the details agree” from “this pack is authentic”, and never claims the second from a lookup alone.
Can Vouch tell me if a medicine is fake?
It can tell you whether the printed number resolves to a registration, whether the product and manufacturer names agree, whether the registration is active, and whether the pack is expired. Those are strong signals. Chemical content and true provenance need laboratory work or a regulator inspection.
What happens if the registry is down?
Vouch says the register could not be reached and marks the affected check as unverifiable. It does not convert an outage into “not registered”, because absence of an answer is not evidence of falsification.
Does my photo leave my device?
In this build, photos are processed by the OCR engine on the machine running Vouch. No model key is configured by default. If you add one, the configured provider receives the image; the app states which reader was used on every extraction.
Is Vouch approved by NAFDAC or FCCPC?
No. Vouch is independent. It reads publicly available data and helps you prepare a report; it does not act for, or carry the endorsement of, any regulator.
What is actually built today?
Scanning (camera or upload), OCR extraction with manual correction, expiry logic, NAFDAC number resolution, barcode lookup, evidence-backed results, case tracking with deadlines, generated report drafts, and a voice agent with a typed fallback. Handheld hardware and spectroscopic analysis are research directions, not features.

Home / How it works

What happens between a photo and a decision

Six steps. Each one is bounded, and each one states its limit. The aim is not a dramatic verdict — it is a decision you can defend.

1 · Capture each side

Start with the front, then turn the pack for the back, the barcode and any seal or damage. The live coach checks sharpness, light and glare; you can rotate the preview in 90° steps. Photos and pasted text work when the camera will not.

2 · Extract and reconcile

Every frame is read separately. Vouch pulls out the registration number, product name, manufacturer, batch, dates and codes, then merges the readings. If two sides disagree, both values stay visible and you decide which to trust.

3 · Inspect the label

You record what you can see: a sticker over print, a second date, a broken seal, a missing hologram, inconsistent typography. These are indicators, not proof.

4 · Check the reference

The registration number is resolved against the NAFDAC register, including numbering variants and likely OCR confusions. Barcodes are validated and looked up in product databases.

5 · Compare the details

Product name, manufacturer and registration status are compared with the pack. A number that resolves to a different product is treated as a serious mismatch.

6 · Present the findings

Each check appears with its evidence, its source, what it proves and what it does not. Actions follow from the findings, not from a score alone.

What no photo can establish. A photograph cannot confirm chemical content, the true manufacturing site, or whether a genuine pack was refilled. Those require laboratory analysis or a regulator inspection. Vouch will not pretend otherwise.
Boundaries

Limits, stated up front

A registration is not a pack

The register confirms that a licence exists. It cannot confirm that the pack in your hand was produced under it.

A barcode is copyable

Printing the same code on a fake is trivial. A database match is a lookup, not a chain of custody.

OCR can misread

0 and O, 1 and I, 5 and S, 8 and B. Vouch shows the extracted text and asks you to confirm it before drawing conclusions.

Public data has gaps

Food and cosmetic registrations are not published in the same register as medicines. Small producers may be missing from barcode databases entirely.

Absence is not guilt

“No record found” produces a reason to escalate, not an accusation. Sellers are not labelled fraudulent from an automated result.

Dates can be altered

A printed date is evidence of what is printed, not of when the pack was filled. That is why label indicators sit beside the date check.

Home / Safety resources

Practical guidance, no fear tactics

Short, usable notes for reading a pack. Written for a shelf, not a classroom.

Finding the expiry date

Tube crimps, bottle bases, sachet seals, carton flaps. Tilt the pack towards the light — the stamp is often the same colour as the surface.

Formats: EXP 06/2028 · 06 2028 · 2028-06 · JUN 28. Without a day, assume the last day of the month.

Best-before vs use-by

Use-by is a safety limit: do not use after it. Best-before is a quality limit: the product may still be safe, but may have changed. Medicines use an expiry date, which is both.

Reading a batch number

Usually 4–10 characters near the date, often after LOT, BATCH or BN. It links a pack to a production run — which is how a recall finds you.

Packaging red flags

A sticker over print. A second date under the first. A broken tamper band. A missing hologram where the brand normally has one. Blurred print. A misspelt brand. Loose tablets.

Each is an indicator. Vouch records it as such and says what it does not prove.

What a barcode proves

It identifies a product record. It does not identify a specific pack, and it is easy to copy. A missing record may mean a small producer, not a fake product.

If you suspect a product

Stop using it. Keep the pack and receipt. Photograph everything before handing anything back. Report to NAFDAC or FCCPC and keep the reference number.

Do not test a suspect medicine on yourself. If you have already taken it and feel unwell, seek medical care and take the pack with you.
Common questions

Short answers

Is an expired medicine always dangerous?
Not always, but its potency and safety can no longer be assured. The further past expiry, the less reliable it is — and liquid medicines, injectables and eye drops carry more risk than a dry tablet.
Can a genuine product have a registration number that does not resolve?
Yes. Punctuation variants, OCR noise and register search quirks can all cause a miss. That is why Vouch tries safe variants and separates “no record in any known format” from “could not ask”.
What does an inactive registration mean?
The licence is no longer active. It does not automatically mean the product is counterfeit; it may be old stock. It is still a reason to stop and ask the seller or the manufacturer.
Where do I report a problem?
NAFDAC for regulated products, and the FCCPC for consumer protection issues. Vouch can draft both from the same evidence and track the deadlines.
Home / About

A verification tool that is honest about its limits

Vouch began with a simple observation: the information needed to protect a shopper often exists, but not where the shopper is standing.

Why we built it

Counterfeit and expired products are usually discussed as a laboratory problem. In practice, most people meet them as a practical one: a pack in a shop, a number they cannot check, and no clear next step if something is wrong.

Vouch joins the check to the follow-through. The result is not the end of the workflow; it is the evidence for the report, the refund demand and the reminder that keeps the case alive.

Principles

  • No false certainty. Four states, explicit evidence, stated limits.
  • Absence is not guilt. A missing record is a reason to escalate, not to accuse.
  • The person is not the product. Sellers are not labelled fraudulent from an automated result.
  • Say what is not built. Planned and research features are labelled as such.

What exists today

  • Working web application and marketing site
  • Camera and photo scanning with on-device OCR
  • Live NAFDAC register lookups
  • Barcode lookups across three open databases
  • Case tracking with follow-up deadlines
  • Draft reports for sellers and regulators

What does not

  • User accounts or cloud storage
  • Business team workspaces
  • Chemical or spectroscopic analysis
  • Any regulatory endorsement
Vouch is an independent project. NAFDAC, FCCPC and the open product databases are data sources, not partners or endorsers.
Home / Privacy

Privacy, in plain language

What this build stores, where, and what it does not do.

Scan history

Checks you run are stored in your browser’s local storage on this device. They are not uploaded to an account, because this build has no accounts. Clearing your browser data removes them.

Photos

Photos are sent to the Vouch server running on your machine for OCR. If a vision model key is configured, the image is sent to that provider; the app names the reader used on every extraction. Without a key, the Windows OCR engine handles it locally.

Cases

Cases are stored in a local database on the Vouch server. Resetting demo cases deletes them permanently.

What we do not do

No advertising trackers. No selling of data. No public case pages. No third-party analytics in this build.

Your controls

Clear history and export your data from Settings in the app.

This is a demonstration build. A production deployment would add accounts, server-side retention rules and an explicit data-processing agreement.

Home / Terms

Terms of use

The short version: Vouch is a decision aid, not an authority.

1 · What Vouch is

Vouch is an informational tool that compares product details against public registers and databases and helps you prepare reports. It is not a laboratory, a regulator, or a substitute for a NAFDAC inspection.

2 · Results are not verdicts

A result describes what the available checks support. “Clear” does not mean authentic. “High-risk signals” does not mean counterfeit. “Inconclusive” means the evidence was insufficient.

3 · Your responsibility

Do not use Vouch to accuse a person or business of a crime. Reports should be filed with the appropriate regulator, based on your own observations and evidence.

4 · Data accuracy

Register and database records can be incomplete, delayed or wrong. Vouch reports what the source returned and when.

5 · Demonstration build

This build runs locally, without accounts or warranties. Use it for evaluation, not as the sole basis for a medical or commercial decision.
